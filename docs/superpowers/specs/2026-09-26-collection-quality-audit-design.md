# Collection Quality Audit — Design

**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Author:** Claude, with Chris
**Status:** Approved for planning

---

## 1. Why

Chris played the Cambridge, MA collection and was served two questions that were
practically the same. That report is the entry point; the audit below establishes
what is actually wrong across all 43 active collections, and three of the four
findings are things a player would hit.

This document covers the audit findings, the decisions taken, and the design for
fixing them. It does **not** cover the Knight Foundation city collections, which
follow this work.

---

## 2. What the audit found

All figures measured against the live database (`kxsdzaojfaibhuzmclfq`) on
2026-09-26, over 3,855 active questions in 43 active collections.

### 2.1 Duplicates — real, concentrated, and already fixed going forward

There are **~126 duplicate pairs that share the same correct answer**. Answer
equality is what distinguishes a true duplicate from a legitimate question family:
`cam-020`/`cam-021` ("what time do polls open" / "close") and `cam-073`/`cam-074`
("role of an Inspector" / "of a Clerk") score high on text similarity but have
different answers and are correctly kept.

Cambridge is the worst collection by a wide margin:

| Collection | same-answer pairs | of which >0.85 similarity |
|---|---|---|
| cambridge-ma | 36 | 7 |
| norwich-uk | 29 | 1 |
| massachusetts-state | 14 | 6 |
| california-state | 12 | 2 |
| plano-tx | 9 | 2 |
| *14 others* | 26 | 3 |

The specific pair Chris most likely hit is an **eight-question cluster** in Cambridge
all asking what voting system the city uses, with the same answer each time:
`cam-030`, `cam-044`, `cam-048`, `cam-052`, `cam-054`, `cam-057`, `cam-064`,
`cam-082`. The worst single pair is `cam-011` / `cam-093` at similarity **1.000** —
"Who appoints **the** members of the Cambridge Board of Election Commissioners?"
versus "Who appoints members of…".

**This is legacy debt, not an active regression.** The five worst collections are the
five oldest. Collections generated after the Phase 57 automatic within-collection
semantic dedup landed — Madison, Milwaukee, Bend — have **zero** same-answer pairs.
The fix works; it was never applied retroactively.

### 2.2 "Easy" does not play easy

| difficulty | active | encounters | % correct | never served |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| easy | 1,061 | 1,237 | **50.0%** | 691 |
| medium | 1,773 | 244 | **50.0%** | 1,594 |
| hard | 1,021 | 247 | 38.5% | 856 |

Easy and medium are statistically indistinguishable. On a four-option question the
no-knowledge floor is 25%, so 50% means players are close to coin-flipping between two
options on questions labelled easy. Chris's instinct that easy questions should be
markedly simpler is supported by the data.

Note the encounter counts: easy's 1,237 is solid, but medium and hard sit near 245
each, so the medium/hard figures carry a band of roughly ±6 points. The easy result
and the easy-versus-hard gap are the trustworthy parts.

### 2.3 Future years offered for events that have already happened

Confirmed, exactly as reported. `wnews-0134` (world-news, generated by the nightly
cron on 2026-09-23):

> *"In what year **was** South African deputy police commissioner Lt Gen Shadrack
> Sibiya **charged** with sexual offences and trafficking?"*
> Options: 2024 / 2025 / 2026 / **2027** — answer 2026.

2027 has not happened. The option tests whether the player parses the question, not
whether they know the answer.

**The defect is narrow and cleanly detectable, because tense decides it.** These are
legitimate and must not be flagged:

- `por-043` — "What year **will** Portland's District 3 and 4 councilors first face
  re-election?" (2025/2026/2027/2028)
- `smo-019` — "What year **is** Santa Monica permitted to close its municipal
  airport?" (2025/2028/2030/2035)
- `ica-100` — "**By what year** does SunLine Transit plan to convert its fleet…"

A past-tense year question offering a year beyond the current one is always wrong.
A future-tense or permissive one is always fine.

### 2.4 Root cause: the repos have diverged and the nightly pipeline runs unguarded

This is the finding that explains why 2.3 shipped, and it is larger than the year bug.

- **`pipelineCron.ts` never calls the quality rules engine.** In
  `ev-accounts/backend/src/trivia/`, only `replacementGenerator.ts`, `routes/admin.ts`
  and `questionQuality/answerPlacement.ts` reference `qualityRules`. Nothing the
  nightly cron generates is checked by `ambiguity`, `structural`, `partisan`,
  `address-phone` or any other rule.
- **`quality-guidelines.ts` has diverged between the repos.** CTC's copy carries a
  §6a on numeric distractors that ev-accounts' copy lacks — and ev-accounts is the
  engine that generates nightly content.

The §6a divergence is measurable in the data. For four-option questions whose options
are all numeric, the rank of the correct value among the four:

| created | n | smallest | 2nd | **3rd** | largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before 2026-09-01 | 1,045 | 22.6% | 26.3% | **26.6%** | 24.5% |
| since 2026-09-01 | 56 | 8.9% | 26.8% | **50.0%** | 14.3% |

The historical bank was fixed and is flat across all four positions. Content generated
since September — which is dominated by the nightly pipeline — has regressed to the
exact flaw §6a was written to prevent: "sort the four numbers, pick the third" scores
50% with no knowledge at all. n=56 is modest, but 50% against an expected 25% is well
beyond chance.

### 2.5 Incidental findings

- **`quality_score` is NULL for 3,567 of 3,855 active questions (92%).** Any rule that
  ranks questions by quality score will fall through for almost the whole bank. Stated
  here because it constrains the dedup keep-rule in §4.1.
- **`duplicate.ts` is not registered in either `index.ts`, but it is not dead code.**
  Its `DuplicateDetector` and `normalizeText` exports are imported directly by the
  active content scripts — `generate-locale-questions.ts`, `generate-state-questions.ts`,
  `content-generation/utils/quality-validation.ts`, `utils/seed-questions.ts` and
  several audit scripts. It simply sits outside the `auditQuestion()` rule chain.
  **ev-accounts has no copy of `duplicate.ts` at all**, which is a second instance of
  the §2.4 divergence. Out of scope here; noted so neither fact is mistaken.
- Sourcing is healthy: 0 active questions lack a source, 0 have an explanation under
  30 characters. The "well sourced" gate in §4.2 is therefore satisfiable everywhere.
- **Two active collections are already below the playbook's 50-question hard floor** —
  `war-in-iran` (33) and `world-news` (44). Both are news-pipeline collections. This
  constrains the dedup purge; see §4.1.
- **`.planning/STATE.md` is stale and should not be trusted for counts.** It says 42
  collections / 41 active and that Climate Agreements "holds 91 active questions".
  Live: **44 collections, 43 active**, and Climate Agreements has **0** active
  questions (1,648 rows linked, none active). `world-news` and `climate-change` are
  both `is_active = true` now, having been created inactive on 2026-09-20. Refreshing
  STATE.md is a small addition to PR 1.

## 3. Decisions

Taken by Chris on 2026-09-26.

### D1 — The easy rubric gains a headline-executive exception, not a blanket reversal

The existing rule in `.planning/COLLECTION-PLAYBOOK.md` §4 — *"An officeholder's name
is never easy"* — **stays true as the general case**. It gains one exception: the
single headline executive of the jurisdiction. Mayor, Governor, President, and Vice
President are easy. Every other named officeholder is not.

Easy additionally gains **term lengths**, **founding/incorporation years**, and a
general test: *the kind of thing you would expect to see in an elementary
history/civics book* — **gated on the question being well sourced**.

This supersedes the 2026-09-20 ruling only in those specific respects. The distractor
test, and "a precise number with close distractors is hard", survive unchanged.

### D2 — The distractor rule is adopted alongside the relabel

Relabelling alone will not move the 50%-correct number, because a question's
difficulty is carried by its option set. An easy question's three distractors must be
ones a resident rules out instantly. Where an otherwise-easy question fails only on
its distractors, **the option set is rewritten rather than the question demoted**.

### D3 — All same-answer duplicates are archived automatically

No per-pair review. Archiving sets `status='archived'` and is fully reversible.

### D4 — Every active question is read individually

Chris chose a full read of all ~3,855 active questions against the revised rubric,
over the cheaper archetype-matching pass.

**Recorded tradeoff:** the Queens NY pilot of 2026-09-20 measured the yield of exactly
this approach — all 63 medium questions read, 8 promoted and 2 demoted, a 13%
promotion rate that moved the collection from 13% to 18% easy and still required 14
new questions to reach the band. The cheaper archetype pass was offered on that basis
and declined. The full read is expected to produce a better-calibrated bank; it should
not be expected to remove the need for the backfill in §4.2.3.

### D5 — The work splits across two pull requests

| PR | Repo | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Civic Trivia Championship | §4.1 dedup purge, §4.2 rubric + relabel + backfill, §4.3 anachronism rule |
| 2 | ev-accounts | §4.4 port §6a, wire the rules engine into `pipelineCron.ts` |

**The year bug persists in production until PR 2 lands.** PR 1 adds the rule that
detects it and cleans existing data, but the nightly cron does not consult the rules
engine, so nothing stops it recurring until PR 2. This is an accepted, explicit
consequence of the split.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Workstream A — duplicate purge

A one-off, reversible data fix.

**Selection.** Within each active collection, take every pair of active questions
where trigram similarity of `text` exceeds 0.55 **and** the correct answer strings are
equal after trimming and lowercasing. Pairs are then resolved into clusters, so the
eight-question Cambridge voting-system group collapses to one survivor rather than
being processed as 28 independent pairs.

**Clustering must not chain transitively without a check.** The news pipeline's
`clusterArticles` bug is the precedent: union-find over pairwise links at a loose
threshold merged eight unrelated stories into one cluster, and the symptom — bigger
clusters — looked like an improvement. The same shape is possible here, where A~B and
B~C at 0.55 but A and C are genuinely different questions.

Two guards, both cheap:

- **Every member of a cluster must share the same answer**, not merely be linked to
  something that does. This alone kills most chaining.
- **Any cluster larger than three is printed in full and eyeballed before the archive
  runs.** From today's numbers that is a handful of clusters, Cambridge's
  voting-system group among them. This is the one place a human look is worth it, and
  it does not reopen D3's per-pair review.

**Keep rule**, applied in order within a cluster:

1. Higher `quality_score` — **decides almost nothing**, since 92% of the bank is NULL;
   NULL sorts last
2. Higher `encounter_count` — keeps the question players have actually seen, so
   existing telemetry stays attached to the survivor
3. Lower `external_id` — deterministic tiebreak

**Application.** Non-survivors get `status='archived'`. Rows in
`trivia.collection_questions` are left in place; the `status='active'` filter on the
serving path already excludes archived questions, and leaving the link rows intact
makes a revert a single `status` update.

**Scope note.** Within-collection only. Cross-collection duplicates are a separate
concern and remain the job of `scan-duplicates.ts`.

**Floor interaction.** The playbook sets a 50-question hard floor, and `war-in-iran`
(33) and `world-news` (44) are already beneath it before anything is archived. Neither
appears in the duplicate table, so the purge is not expected to touch them — but the
check must be "no collection is pushed *further* below the floor", not "every
collection clears 50", or the purge will appear to fail on a pre-existing condition it
did not cause. Those two collections' thinness is a pipeline-yield question, separate
from this work.

**Verification.** Re-run the selection query afterwards and confirm it returns zero
same-answer pairs; confirm no collection dropped below the floor that was not already
below it; record before/after active counts per collection.

### 4.2 Workstream B — difficulty rubric, relabel, and backfill

#### 4.2.1 Rewrite the rubric

`.planning/COLLECTION-PLAYBOOK.md` §4 is revised per D1 and D2. The blanket line *"An
officeholder's name is never easy"* becomes the headline-executive exception. The
HARD bullet *"Named holders of offices below the headline ones"* already anticipates
this distinction and stays as written.

The same guidance is mirrored into the two generation prompts in CTC
(`prompts/system-prompt.ts`, `prompts/state-system-prompt.ts`) so newly generated
collections are labelled correctly at the source. The ev-accounts copies are PR 2.

#### 4.2.2 The relabel pass

Every active question is read against the revised rubric and assigned a revised
difficulty plus a **reason code**, so the pass is auditable and its yield measurable:

| code | meaning | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `HEADLINE_EXEC` | Mayor / Governor / President / VP | → easy |
| `TERM_LENGTH` | length of a term of office | → easy |
| `FOUNDING_YEAR` | founding, incorporation, chartering year | → easy |
| `ELEM_CIVICS` | elementary civics-book fact, well sourced | → easy |
| `LANDMARK_GEO` | recognisable landmark, orientation, geography | → easy |
| `SUB_OFFICEHOLDER` | named holder below the headline office | → not easy |
| `PRECISE_NUMBER` | exact figure with close distractors | → hard |
| `DISTRACTOR_FAIL` | easy subject, four plausible options | → rewrite options (D2) |
| `NO_CHANGE` | label already correct | — |

Results are written to `.planning/difficulty-audit/` as one JSON file per collection —
`external_id`, old label, new label, reason code — mirroring the existing
`.planning/dedup-reports/` convention. The pass is **resumable per collection**, which
matters at this size, and the files are the review artifact and the input to the
`UPDATE` batches.

`DISTRACTOR_FAIL` questions get their option set rewritten. The correct value is never
moved; only the distractors change. Rewrites must respect §6a — vary which bracket the
answer falls in, one unit per question, options ordered ascending.

#### 4.2.3 Backfill to the band

After relabelling, any collection still under 25% easy gets new easy questions written
until it clears 25%, aiming at the 25–33% band.

**25% is a floor, not a band to sit inside.** Chris's rule is "*at least* 25-33% easy".
Seven collections are already above 33% — `world-news` (52.3%), `indiana-state`
(42.9%), `california-state` (40.7%), `norwich-uk` (39.3%), `milwaukee-wi` (38.9%),
`cambridge-ma` (38.7%), `wisconsin` (34.4%) — and the relabel in §4.2.2 will push more
of them there, since it promotes three whole archetypes into easy. **Nothing is
demoted to bring a collection back down under 33%.** The only demotions are the ones
the rubric itself calls for: `DISTRACTOR_FAIL`, `SUB_OFFICEHOLDER`, `PRECISE_NUMBER`.

Batch size is `(0.25 * total - easy) / 0.75`, **not** `0.25 * total - easy` — adding
questions raises the denominator too.

Eighteen collections are under 25% today, the thinnest being `biloxi-ms` (16.4%),
`new-york-state` (17.2%) and `mississippi-state` (17.4%). The relabel will move some of
these into the band on its own; the backfill list is computed *after* the relabel, not
from today's figures.

Per the playbook, the productive seams for new easy questions are orientation and
geography, the single most recognisable landmark or institution, and — for state
collections — state-scale natural features. State collections stay state-scale: the
capital city is allowed as the seat of state institutions, city landmarks are not.

All new questions go through `placeAnswer()`, which is mandatory on any question-insert
path.

#### 4.2.4 Verification

- Every active collection is at or above 25% easy; none demoted merely to stay under 33%
- Reason-code counts reported per collection, so D4's yield is measured against the
  Queens pilot's 13% rather than assumed
- No collection loses questions; totals only rise
- Spot-check that `HEADLINE_EXEC` promotions really are the headline executive and not
  a deputy or a council president

### 4.3 Workstream C — the anachronism rule

A new **blocking** rule, `rules/anachronism.ts`, registered in
`services/qualityRules/index.ts` alongside the existing rules.

**Logic.** If the question asks for a year or date *and* is phrased in the past tense,
no option may be a year later than the current year.

- Past-tense triggers: `was`, `were`, `did`, `has been`, `became`, `opened`,
  `established`, `founded`, `signed`, `charged`
- Explicit exemptions: `will`, `by what year`, `is … permitted`, `plans to`,
  `projected`, `expects`, `target` — any forward-looking construction
- "Current year" is read from the clock at evaluation time, not hardcoded

**Severity is blocking**, consistent with broken Learn More links: a question offering
an impossible answer is a correctness defect, not a style issue.

**Tests.** The rule ships with fixtures drawn from the real bank — `wnews-0134` must
fail; `por-043`, `smo-019` and `ica-100` must pass. These are the cases that make the
tense distinction load-bearing, so they are the ones worth pinning.

**Backfill.** Run the rule across the existing active bank and fix what it catches.
Today's scan surfaces one clear violation (`wnews-0134`), which suggests the historical
damage is small — but the scan I ran was a hand-written regex, and the rule will be
stricter, so the real count is established by running it.

**Placement note — the rule genuinely belongs in both repos.**

CTC's `backend/` is frozen per ev-cto decision 0013, and `backend/FROZEN.md` is blunt
that changes here do not reach production. But `backend/src/services/qualityRules/` is
a **shared dependency of the collection-creation flow, which FROZEN.md explicitly keeps
active in this repo.** `content-generation/utils/quality-validation.ts` calls
`auditQuestion()`, and `generate-locale-questions.ts` and `generate-state-questions.ts`
import from the same tree. So a rule added here does real work: it guards every new
collection at creation time, including the Knight Foundation cities.

It does not guard the nightly pipeline, which is PR 2.

**This means the rule ships twice, and that is the §2.4 failure mode by construction.**
The mitigation is not to pick one repo — both paths need the guard — but to land PR 2
promptly and to treat `anachronism.ts` and `quality-guidelines.ts` as a named
sync pair in both PRs' descriptions. PR 1 must not be merged and left to sit.

### 4.4 Workstream D — close the divergence (PR 2, ev-accounts)

Deferred by D5, specified here so it is not lost.

1. Port §6a (numeric distractors) from CTC's `quality-guidelines.ts` into
   `ev-accounts/backend/src/trivia/scripts/content-generation/prompts/quality-guidelines.ts`
2. Port the revised §4 difficulty rubric into the ev-accounts generation prompts
3. Port `anachronism.ts` and register it
4. **Wire the rules engine into the nightly pipeline's write path** —
   `scripts/international/question-generator.ts` is where the news lanes write, and
   where `wnews-0134` originated
5. Re-measure the numeric-distractor distribution after a week of nightly runs; the
   "since 2026-09-01" row should converge on 25% per bracket

Chris's stated preference at decision time was a flagged rollout for the pipeline
wiring — log violations first, enforce after observing a night or two — and that
should carry into PR 2's own plan.

## 6. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Dedup archives a question that was legitimately distinct | Answer equality is required, not just text similarity; `status='archived'` is a one-column revert |
| Transitive clustering chains unrelated questions into one cluster, as `clusterArticles` did with news stories | Same-answer required of every cluster member, not just linked pairs; clusters larger than three printed and eyeballed before archiving (§4.1) |
| The full read (D4) is long and may drift in judgement across 3,855 questions | Per-collection resumable JSON artifacts with reason codes; the codes make drift visible and re-checkable |
| Relabelling alone fails to move the 50%-correct figure | D2's distractor rewrites attack the actual cause; the measurement is re-run after the pass |
| The anachronism rule blocks legitimate forward-looking questions | Explicit exemption list, plus real-bank fixtures pinning `por-043`, `smo-019`, `ica-100` as passing |
| Backfill questions repeat existing ones | The §4.1 selection query is re-run after the backfill, not only before |
| The year bug recurs nightly until PR 2 | Accepted under D5 and stated plainly; PR 2 should follow promptly |

---

## 7. Success criteria

1. Zero same-answer duplicate pairs within any active collection
2. Every active collection at or above 25% easy
3. `anachronism.ts` registered, blocking, green against its fixtures, and the existing
   bank clean by its own measure
4. Playbook §4 and both CTC generation prompts state the revised rubric
5. Reason-code yield reported, so D4's cost is on the record for the next collection
6. PR 2 specified and ready to plan

## 8. Deployment note

Every change here is documentation, content scripts, backend rules, and database rows.
Nothing touches `frontend/`, and the Render static site filters deploys by that
directory both ways — so **PR 1 creates no production deploy at all**, not a slow one.

The database changes are decoupled from that entirely: they are applied directly to the
shared Supabase project via SQL, so they take effect **when they are applied, not when
the PR merges**, and they reach production immediately because production reads the
same database. The dedup purge and the relabel are therefore live changes to what
players see, and should be treated with more care than the docs-only file diff suggests.

Per CLAUDE.md, both PRs go through the normal review gate. The admin bypass on
`master` is not used, and CI job names are not renamed.
